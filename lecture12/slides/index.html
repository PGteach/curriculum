1-1 · Development of Information Technology and Social Transformation

Information technology developed in stages — computers, the Internet, smartphones, and cloud computing. At each stage it introduced a new technology or service and also changed how society communicates, works, learns, and pays.

1-2 · How AI Works

AI is the broad field; machine learning sits inside it, deep learning inside machine learning, and today's generative AI is built on deep learning — from the broadest idea to the most specialized.

1-3 · AI in Daily Life and Industry

AI is good at finding and classifying patterns in data — recommendations, voice, translation, face recognition, and many industry tasks — but human judgment is still needed where ethics, privacy, and responsibility are involved.

1-4 · Ethical Issues with AI

If training data is biased, an AI can reproduce that bias; using AI responsibly means checking for bias, being able to explain decisions, and knowing who is accountable — guided by fairness, transparency, privacy protection, and accountability.

1-1 · Exam traps

True or false?

  • E-commerce means buying goods in physical stores with cash. — false: It is buying and selling through the Internet.
  • VR overlays digital information on real-world images. — false: That is AR. VR replaces the real world with a virtual one.
  • Moore's Law has been said to be approaching a physical limit in recent years. — true: Transistors are now so small that problems like leakage current appear.
  • The correct order is: computer → smartphones → Internet → cloud. — false: Computer → Internet → smartphones → cloud.

1-1 · Exam-style question · 6 marks

Plan it, then check the points

Analyse how the spread of cloud computing (from the 2010s onward) has changed the way information technology is used. Refer to: large-scale data analysis, AI, and "IT as a service".

  • Point 1 — Cloud computing spread from the 2010s onward.
  • Point 2 — It made large-scale data analysis possible, because storage and processing power can be rented rather than owned.
  • Point 3 — That data and power in turn support AI.
  • Point 4 — IT became something delivered as a service over the Internet, rather than hardware each organisation buys and maintains.
  • Point 5 — Effect: smaller organisations and individuals can now use computing power that previously needed a large investment.

1-2 · Exam traps

True or false?

  • Deep learning is a completely different technology from machine learning. — false: Deep learning is a type of machine learning.
  • AI and machine learning have the same meaning. — false: AI is the broad concept; machine learning is one technology inside it.
  • Generative AI uses deep learning to generate new data. — true: That is its definition.
  • Machine learning follows only fixed rules written by people. — false: It learns patterns from data.

Decide first, then tap.

1-2 · Exam-style question · 6 marks

Plan it, then check the points

Analyse the relationship between AI, machine learning, deep learning and generative AI, with an example of each.

  • Point 1 — AI is the broad field: technologies that perform intelligent human behaviour on a computer. Example: speech recognition.
  • Point 2 — Machine learning sits inside AI: it learns patterns from data to make predictions and judgments. Example: a spam filter.
  • Point 3 — Deep learning sits inside machine learning: it uses neural networks and large-scale data. Example: image analysis for autonomous driving.
  • Point 4 — Generative AI is built on deep learning and generates new data. Example: ChatGPT.
  • Point 5 — The four are nested categories, each more specialised than the one before, not four separate technologies.

Source: p.16 — Exam-style question [6]. Each point is about one mark.

1-3 · Exam traps

True or false?

  • AI is good at high-speed processing of large amounts of data. — true: One of its strengths, with recognising images and audio.
  • AI can be left to make ethical judgments on its own. — false: Ethical judgments need human judgment: they may carry discrimination or prejudice.
  • The black-box problem means it is clear how an AI reached its judgment. — false: It means it is unclear.
  • Predicting harvest timing and detecting pests is an AI use in agriculture. — true: From the book's table of industries.

1-3 · Exam-style question · 6 marks

Plan it, then check the points

Analyse why AI is well suited to some tasks but requires caution in others.

  • Point 1 — AI is good at finding and classifying patterns in complex data such as images and text.
  • Point 2 — It is good at recognition and generation of images, audio and text.
  • Point 3 — It is good at probabilistic reasoning and prediction from data.
  • Point 4 — Caution: ethical judgments may carry discrimination or prejudice.
  • Point 5 — Caution: anything involving personal data raises privacy questions.
  • Point 6 — Caution: final decision-making — someone has to be responsible for the result.

Source: p.22 — Exam-style question [6]. Each point is about one mark.

1-4 · Exam traps

True or false?

  • Algorithmic bias is caused by the AI's processing speed. — false: It comes from bias in the training data.
  • If an AI's process is opaque, there is no problem as long as the result is correct. — false: An opaque result cannot be checked.
  • Who is responsible when an AI is wrong has already been clearly decided. — false: No clear standard exists yet among developers, users and operators.
  • Bias in training data can cause discriminatory bias in AI judgments. — true: That is exactly algorithmic bias.

Decide first, then tap.

1-4 · Exam-style question · 6 marks

Plan it, then check the points

Analyze how algorithmic bias can arise in an AI system, and how it can be reduced, using the principles of AI ethics.

  • Point 1 — Cause: the AI learns from training data
  • Point 2 — Biased or insufficient data is reproduced in its judgments, with an example
  • Point 3 — A second source: past discrimination in data, or proxy variable / variable choice
  • Point 4 — Reduction through fairness: check data, test results across groups
  • Point 5 — Reduction through transparency: show the decision process / XAI
  • Point 6 — Links the measures back to the cause, so the answer is an analysis, not a list

Source: p.30 — Exam-style question [6]. Each point is about one mark.

All 4 lessons · quick-fire

Mixed questions

  • Which came first: the Internet or the smartphone? — The Internet (1990s); smartphones came in the 2000s.
  • Why does a self-driving car use edge computing? — A delay of even 0.1 seconds can cause an accident, so it decides on the car itself.
  • Is deep learning a kind of machine learning? — Yes: it sits inside machine learning.
  • A spam filter that learns from examples is… — machine learning.
  • A video site suggests your next clip. Which AI? — A recommendation system.
  • Why must a human keep the final decision on a diagnosis? — Someone has to be responsible; AI can be a black box and can be wrong.
  • A hiring AI rates one group lower. Which principle is broken? — Fairness.
  • Showing users in plain language how the AI decides is… — transparency.

أسئلة من كل الدروس متلخبطة — زي الامتحان. فكّر وبعدين دوس.
