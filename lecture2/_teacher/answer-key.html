3You were writing on your laptop for an hour. The battery died before you pressed Save, and your work was gone.
  • It was in RAM.
  • RAM only holds data while the power is on, so when the battery died the unsaved work was wiped. Saving copies it to storage, which keeps it.

10In autonomous driving, why is it necessary to process data instantly on the vehicle side using edge computing, rather than sending the data to the cloud for judgment? Explain your answer.
p.8 — Pause & Think prompt (textbook wording)
  • model — Because a delay of even 0.1 seconds can lead to an accident. Sending the data to the cloud and waiting for an answer takes too long, so the decision must be made instantly on the vehicle itself using edge computing.
  • marking — Full marks need the timing point (a fraction of a second matters) AND the conclusion that the round trip to the cloud is too slow. Half marks for only saying 'it is faster'.

12Analyse how the spread of cloud computing (from the 2010s onward) has changed the way information technology is used. In your answer, refer to: large-scale data analysis, AI, and "IT as a service".
p.10 — Exam-style question, [6] marks, textbook wording
  • marks — 6
  • points — Cloud computing spread from the 2010s onward., It made large-scale data analysis possible, because storage and processing power can be rented rather than owned., That data and power in turn support AI., IT became something delivered as a service over the Internet, rather than hardware each organisation buys and maintains., Effect: smaller organisations and individuals can now use computing power that previously needed a large investment.
  • marking — 2 marks per required term (large-scale data analysis, AI, IT as a service) when it is explained rather than just named.
13Choose one emerging technology from this lesson (autonomous driving, AR, VR, or quantum computing). Suggest one way it could help solve a real problem, and state one risk.
p.12 — Reflect & Challenge
  • open — True
  • marking — Accept any of the four technologies. Look for three things: the technology named correctly, a realistic problem it addresses, and a risk that genuinely follows from that technology.
  • example — Autonomous driving could help elderly or disabled people travel independently. The risk is that the system may misjudge a situation it was never trained on, so some human oversight is still needed.
