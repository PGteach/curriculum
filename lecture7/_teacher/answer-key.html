5Why must containment come before eradication in the six-stage response?
p.50 — Pause & Think
[3 marks]

Model answer. Containment limits the spread of the damage. While the cause is being removed, it could keep spreading — for example, malware moving to other computers on the network. So the infected part is isolated first, and then the cause is removed safely.

The answer must cover:

  • Containment stops the damage spreading (1)
  • During eradication the threat could otherwise keep spreading (1)
  • So isolate first, then remove the cause (1)
6Explain why the six stages of incident response must be carried out in order, using recovery and eradication as an example. Refer to what happens if the cause is not removed.
p.51 — Exam-style question [6]
[6 marks]

Model answer. Each stage depends on the one before it. Containment comes first, to stop the damage spreading; eradication then removes the cause, such as the malware; only then does recovery return the systems to normal. If recovery is done before eradication, the systems are restored while the cause is still there — the malware or the weakness it used — so the same incident can happen again, and the work has to be repeated. Following the order keeps the damage small and stops the incident returning. In practice some stages can overlap: recovery may reveal traces of the threat, and the team goes back to containment or eradication.

The answer must cover:

  • Containment first stops the damage spreading (1)
  • Eradication removes the cause, e.g. the malware (1)
  • Recovery returns systems to normal only after that (1)
  • If recovery comes before eradication, the cause is still there (1)
  • So the same incident can happen again (1)
  • Conclusion: order keeps damage small / may need to step back (1)

11Discuss the importance of the lessons-learned-and-improvement stage, and how it helps prevent security incidents from happening again.
Ministry assessments, form B — essay 3
[4 marks]

Model answer. Lessons learned and improvement is the last stage. The team analyzes what happened and how the attacker got in, documents the lessons, and improves the controls — for example patching the weakness, adding a protection, or changing a procedure. Without it, the same weakness stays and the same incident can return. With it, the incident becomes less likely to happen again, and its effect smaller if it does.

The answer must cover:

  • Analyze what happened / the cause (1)
  • Document the lessons (1)
  • Improve the controls (1)
  • So the same incident is less likely to happen again (1)
12Explain how the recovery stage and the lessons-learned-and-improvement stage are carried out when dealing with a ransomware attack.
Ministry assessments, form C — essay 1
[4 marks]

Model answer. Recovery: once the ransomware has been removed, the encrypted systems are restored to a safe, normal state from a clean backup, and checked before they are used again. Lessons learned and improvement: the team finds how the ransomware got in, documents it, and improves the controls — for example updating the software it exploited, training staff, and keeping regular backups — so it does not happen again.

The answer must cover:

  • Recovery comes after the ransomware is removed (1)
  • Restore from a clean backup to a normal, safe state (1)
  • Lessons learned: find how it got in and document it (1)
  • Improve the controls so it does not happen again (1)
