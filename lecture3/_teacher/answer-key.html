11Analyse the relationship between AI, machine learning, deep learning and generative AI. Use nested categories and give one example of each.
p.16 — Exam-style question
[6 marks]

The answer must cover:

  • AI is the broad field: technologies that perform intelligent human behaviour on a computer. Example: speech recognition.
  • Machine learning sits inside AI: it learns patterns from data to make predictions and judgments. Example: a spam filter.
  • Deep learning sits inside machine learning: it uses neural networks and large-scale data. Example: image analysis for autonomous driving.
  • Generative AI is built on deep learning and generates new data. Example: ChatGPT.
  • The four are nested categories, each more specialised than the one before, not four separate technologies.

Marking. 4 marks for the four layers in the right order with an example each, 2 for stating that they are nested rather than separate. A list with no nesting statement scores 4.

16Analyse why AI is well suited to some tasks but requires caution in others. Refer to what AI is good at (finding patterns, recognition, prediction) and to what needs caution (ethical judgments, privacy, final decision-making).
p.22 — Exam-style question
[6 marks]

The answer must cover:

  • AI is good at finding and classifying patterns in complex data such as images and text.
  • It is good at recognition and generation of images, audio and text.
  • It is good at probabilistic reasoning and prediction from data.
  • Caution: ethical judgments may carry discrimination or prejudice.
  • Caution: anything involving personal data raises privacy questions.
  • Caution: final decision-making — someone has to be responsible for the result.

Marking. 3 marks for the strengths, 3 for the cautions. A list with no explanation of WHY each one follows scores half.

Prepared by: Mr. Eissa Islam
17A hospital wants an image-diagnosis AI to detect disease from X-ray images. Explain why the final diagnosis should still be confirmed by a human doctor rather than left to the AI alone.
p.21 — Think It Through

The answer must cover:

  • A wrong diagnosis can seriously harm a patient, so responsibility matters.
  • The black-box problem: it is not clear how the AI reached its judgment.
  • Training data may be biased, making the judgment less accurate for some patients.
  • Final decision-making is exactly what the book lists as needing caution.

Marking. 2 marks for a harm/responsibility point, 2 for a limitation of the AI itself. Naming 'black box' without explaining it scores 1.

Prepared by: Mr. Eissa Islam
18Generative AI can produce text that sounds plausible but is factually incorrect — a hallucination. Explain why it is dangerous to hand such output in as a school report as it is, and state what you should do instead.
p.16 — Think It Through

The answer must cover:

  • A hallucination reads as confidently as a correct answer, so the reader cannot tell.
  • The student is responsible for what they hand in, not the tool.
  • An error passed on as fact can mislead whoever reads the report.
  • What to do instead: check every fact against the textbook or another reliable source before using it.

Marking. 2 marks for why it is dangerous, 2 for a concrete checking step. Naming 'hallucination' alone is not an explanation.

Prepared by: Mr. Eissa Islam
