4Explain the main causes of algorithmic bias. Give one cause that comes from the training data and one that comes from the way the system is built.
p.27 — main causes of bias; ministry assessment, lesson 4

The answer must cover:

  • From the data (either): insufficient data on certain attributes; or past discriminatory tendencies reflected in the data.
  • From the way it is built (any one): an inappropriate choice of variables; a proxy variable that indirectly stands in for a protected attribute; the design of the model itself.

Marking. 2 marks each side: 1 for naming the cause, 1 for saying how it leads to biased judgments.

6Answer about explainable AI.
p.28 — XAI; p.29 Worked Example (1) B

The answer must cover:

  • XAI: technology that makes it possible for humans to understand why an AI made a particular judgment.
  • If the process is opaque, nobody can verify whether the result is correct — a right answer by chance cannot be told apart from a wrong one, and an unfair decision cannot be found or challenged.

Marking. 2 marks for the definition; 3 for the explanation, which must mention verification.

7Answer about responsibility and accountability.
p.28 — Responsibility; ministry assessment, lesson 4

The answer must cover:

  • The developer, the user and the operator.
  • Responsibility is the roles and duties of each party — who does what, and who answers when a judgment is wrong. Accountability is being answerable for the AI's decisions: knowing who can be held to account, according to those roles. Accountability is one of the four principles.

Marking. 1 mark for the three parties; 3 for a clear difference, not two restated definitions of the same thing.

9Complete the table: what each principle means, and one situation where it applies.
p.28 — basic principles of AI ethics
  • Fairness — not unjustly discriminating against any particular person or group — e.g. a hiring AI evaluating fairly regardless of gender
  • Transparency — showing the decision-making process and inner workings clearly — e.g. telling users in plain language how the AI decides
  • Privacy protection — handling personal information appropriately — e.g. not using collected data for another purpose
  • Accountability — being answerable for the AI's decisions — e.g. someone answers when a diagnostic result is wrong

10Analyze how algorithmic bias can arise in an AI system, and how it can be reduced. In your answer, refer to the cause of bias (biased or insufficient training data) and to how the principles of fairness and transparency help address it.
p.30 — Exam-style question [6]
[6 marks]

Model answer. Algorithmic bias arises because an AI learns its patterns from training data. If that data is biased — for example, it contains too little data on certain groups, or it reflects past discrimination — the AI reproduces the same bias in its judgments. Bias can also enter through the way the system is built, such as a proxy variable that stands in for a protected attribute. It can be reduced by checking the training data for missing or unfair patterns and testing the AI's results across different groups, which applies the principle of fairness; and by making the decision-making process visible, for example with explainable AI, which applies the principle of transparency and lets people find and correct unfair decisions.

The answer must cover:

  • Cause: the AI learns from training data (1)
  • Biased or insufficient data is reproduced in its judgments, with an example (1)
  • A second source: past discrimination in data, or proxy variable / variable choice (1)
  • Reduction through fairness: check data, test results across groups (1)
  • Reduction through transparency: show the decision process / XAI (1)
  • Links the measures back to the cause, so the answer is an analysis, not a list (1)

Marking. Six points, one mark each. A list of the four principles with no link to bias earns no more than 2.

11A hiring AI was found to rate applicants of a particular gender lower. Using algorithmic bias and the principles of AI ethics, explain the most likely cause and one possible measure to address it.
p.31 — Think It Through
[4 marks]
